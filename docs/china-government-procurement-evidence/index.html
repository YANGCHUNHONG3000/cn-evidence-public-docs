Use case · Government procurement

Check a Chinese Supplier’s Government Procurement Evidence

Procurement awards can show a supplier’s recorded activity with public buyers. They are a useful trail to investigate—not a certificate of successful delivery.

Inspect OpenAPIConnect Remote MCP

Remote MCP is a Streamable HTTP endpoint for client configuration, not a browser search form.

Read an award as a recorded event, not a performance verdict

China government procurement records can help an agent examine reported buyer relationships, bid awards and tender history. Chinese supplier contract awards may identify an announcement, date, buyer or amount when those fields exist in the source. Missing fields should remain missing.

Awards are not proof of contract performance. An award does not establish delivery quality, acceptance, payment completion or the absence of a later dispute. Verify those separately before treating a supplier as proven.

  1. Confirm the legal company first. Similar names in a procurement announcement are not enough to attribute an award automatically.
  2. Inspect procurement summaries alongside linkage and dataset_coverage. The currently documented procurement source is Beijing award data, not every China tender.
  3. When a decision needs supporting records, request Full and preserve content_id, announcement or lot identifiers and source_files when present. These traceable sources help a reviewer locate the underlying material.

For supplier due diligence, an aggregate award count is a screening signal. Procurement evidence should retain the original source context rather than become an unsupported “performed successfully” label.

Keep source scope attached to the conclusion

Results are limited to configured official/open datasets and must not be interpreted as nationwide completeness. No procurement record found is not proof of no awards elsewhere. A content_id locates the dataset; preserve finer announcement/record locators when available. x402 pay-per-call lets an agent request detail for the cases that need it, with spending authorization—not a prepaid account.
